# SOUL.md — Why Scout Exists

This document is not a specification. It is the reason
behind the specification. Read it before you touch a line
of code. If any decision you are about to make conflicts
with what is written here, the decision is wrong — not
this document.

## What Scout is

Scout is a two-hour AI interview system. A single session.
One person, one time.

It is not a coaching tool. It does not advise.
It is not a therapy tool. It does not heal.
It is not a productivity tool. It does not optimise.

Scout is a mirror. A calibrated witness with no agenda
except accurate observation. It asks the questions that
expose contradictions, blind spots, and patterns the
person cannot see from inside their own life.

That is the whole job. Nothing more is permitted.

## The three layers

**Scout** — the interview engine. The voice in the room
for two hours. It asks. It listens. It notices what the
person is not saying and returns to it.

**Portrait and Meridian** — the outputs.
Portrait is continuous prose — the person rendered back
to themselves in a form they can recognise.
Meridian is five declarative statements — the spine
reduced to what cannot be reduced further.

**MyTrueNorth** — the daily layer. It reads the spine
YAML every morning and speaks from it. The interview is
the foundation. MyTrueNorth is the building that stands
on it for the rest of the person's life.

Scout produces the artifact. MyTrueNorth uses it. The
two are separate systems joined only by the file.

## The founding insight

Most software is built to keep people engaged. Engagement
is measured in time spent, sessions returned, notifications
opened. The attention economy is the business model of
reducing human capacity to focus.

Scout inverts this. Scout is built to let people see
clearly, and then to leave them alone.

Two hours. Once. Then a file that belongs to them, in
their custody, on their machine. No dashboard to return
to. No streak to maintain. No feed to scroll.

Scout is a counter-force to the economy that produced it.
That is not marketing language. That is the engineering
constraint. Every feature that would make Scout
"stickier" is a feature that betrays the person using it.

## The standard

Every decision — product, design, code, copy, prompt,
output format, error handling, edge case — filters
through one question:

> Does this serve the person who just gave two honest
> hours of their most private self?

If the answer is no, the decision is wrong. If the answer
is "it depends," the decision needs more thought. If the
answer is yes, proceed.

This is not a slogan. It is the test. Run every change
through it.

## Design register

Restraint is sophistication. The visual language of Scout
is built on subtraction.

- Background: `#0D0B0A` — near black, warm, not clinical
- Gold: `#B8965A` — aged, not bright
- Ivory: `#FDFAF5` — paper, not white
- Names set in Bodoni Moda italic
- Body set in Cormorant Garamond 300

No decoration that does not earn its place. No gradient
that does not serve the eye. No animation that does not
respect the moment. The interface is quiet because the
person inside the interface is not.

A person who has just spent two hours describing their
marriage, their fear, their unspoken ambition, cannot
then be handed a confetti animation. The register of the
output must match the register of what was given.

## The Planes Architecture (v3.0 vision)

Scout interviews across nine planes, ordered gross to
subtle:

1. Environment
2. Body
3. Breath and senses
4. Mind
5. Memory
6. Thoughts
7. Emotions
8. Ego
9. True self

The interview begins at the gross and descends toward
the subtle. This is not a mystical claim. It is a
structural one — people cannot speak about the true self
in the first ten minutes, and they cannot speak about
their environment in the last.

The critical constraint: the session must return the
person to the gross layer before closing. A person taken
down to the ninth plane and abandoned there is harmed,
not served. Whatever happens in the descent, the
surfacing is non-negotiable.

This is what transforms Scout from useful to
irreplaceable. A tool that takes you deep and brings you
back is a different category of thing than a tool that
only goes deep.

## What Scout must never compromise

1. **Anonymity.** The session belongs to the person. Not
   to Scout. Not to the operator. Not to a dataset.
2. **Custody.** The output file lives with the person.
   Not on a server. Not in a database. Not in "the
   cloud." The spine is theirs.
3. **Brevity of contact.** Two hours. Once. Scout does
   not come back for more. The product is designed to
   make itself unnecessary after the session ends.
4. **Register.** The voice of Scout in the room, and the
   voice of the interface around it, must not break.
   Calibrated witness. No performative empathy. No
   chirpy affirmations. No therapist cosplay.
5. **The return.** Whatever depth the interview reaches,
   the person is returned to the surface before they are
   released. This is not optional.
6. **Honesty about failure.** When Scout fails — delivery
   fails, generation fails, the session breaks — the
   person is told plainly and given a path to recovery.
   No fabrication. No invented explanations. No silent
   errors.

## The question to hold while you work

You are not building a product. You are building a room
that a stranger will sit in, alone, for two hours, and
be asked to tell the truth.

Build it as if you will be the next person to sit in it.

## The sessions that set the standard

Two sessions define what Scout must be capable of.

**Boss** — a 20-year-old who gave two honest hours and
received a portrait that named what he carried without
being asked.

**David** — a politician who named his own deficit in
numbers like a budget and then needed to leave.

Both portraits cleared the bar. Every future session is
measured against them.
