Technically

Scout is an AI interviewer. It will ask you questions — one at a time, carefully — about who you are, what you value, what you are building, and what you are afraid of.

At the end, it will produce a structured document called a spine — a personal constitution that captures the real version of you, not the performed one.

The conversation takes between 30 and 60 minutes. There are no right answers. There is no scoring. Nothing you say here is stored after your session ends. The spine belongs to you alone.

If your connection drops or your browser closes mid-session, your progress is not lost. Your session is held in a temporary file — like a document that saves itself while you write. When you return with the same key, Scout picks up exactly where you left off. The moment your spine is delivered and you close this window, that file is deleted permanently. Nothing is kept after that point. Nothing.

You will need your single-use key to begin. It expires the moment your spine is delivered. There are no second sessions.

With truth

This will only work if you are honest. Not performatively honest — not the version of honesty that sounds good. The kind that costs something to say out loud.

Scout will not judge you. It will not give you advice. It will not tell you what to do. It will listen with more precision than most humans are capable of, and it will reflect back what it hears — including the things you did not quite say.

The people who get the most from this are the ones who stop editing themselves. The ones who say the thing they have never said to anyone. The ones who let the silence sit rather than filling it with noise.

If you are ready for that — enter your key.
